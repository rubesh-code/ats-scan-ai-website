How to Improve Your ATS Score Without Keyword Stuffing

A better score usually comes from a more relevant and readable resume—not from repeating every word in a job description.

ATS Scan AI Editorial TeamUpdated 5 August 20268 min read
Key point

A better score usually comes from a more relevant and readable resume—not from repeating every word in a job description.

Start with the target role

An ATS-style comparison only makes sense when the resume is evaluated against a specific role. Read the job description and separate requirements into skills, tools, responsibilities, domain knowledge, and experience level. Prioritize items that appear repeatedly or are described as required.

Fix missing essentials first

Before rewriting every bullet, confirm the resume has standard headings, consistent dates, recognizable job titles, and selectable text. A visually impressive design is not helpful when important text is difficult to parse or understand.

Add keywords with evidence

A keyword is strongest when it appears in a sentence that proves experience. Instead of listing “SQL” alone, describe the query, data size, report, or decision it supported. Do not claim tools or responsibilities you have not used.

Strengthen achievement bullets

Use a simple pattern: action, task, method, and outcome. Outcomes can include time saved, errors reduced, revenue influenced, users supported, performance improved, or delivery accelerated. Use numbers only when they are accurate.

Remove low-value content

Generic objectives, long skill lists, repeated responsibilities, and unrelated experience can dilute the role match. Keep useful context, but give the most space to evidence that supports the target position.

Review the report details

Do not optimize only for the total score. Check which keywords are missing, whether the format is readable, and whether important experience is described clearly. A slightly lower score with honest evidence is better than an inflated score based on unsupported claims.
