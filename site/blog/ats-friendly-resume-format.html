ATS-Friendly Resume Format: A Practical Guide

An ATS-friendly format is simple enough for software to parse and clear enough for a recruiter to scan quickly.

ATS Scan AI Editorial TeamUpdated 5 August 20267 min read
Key point

An ATS-friendly format is simple enough for software to parse and clear enough for a recruiter to scan quickly.

Use a straightforward layout

A single-column layout is the safest starting point. Two-column layouts can work, but they increase the chance that text order becomes unclear when extracted. Avoid placing essential information in decorative sidebars.

Choose standard section headings

Use headings such as Summary, Skills, Experience, Projects, Education, and Certifications. Creative headings may look distinctive but can make the section purpose less obvious.

Keep contact details in normal text

Place your name, email, phone, location, and portfolio links near the top of the document. Avoid relying on headers, footers, icons, or images for essential details.

Use readable typography

Choose common fonts, clear sizes, consistent spacing, and strong contrast. Avoid very small text, excessive capitalization, and dense paragraphs. Bullet points should usually be easier to review than large blocks of text.

Export a text-based file

After exporting to PDF, try selecting and copying the text. If the resume behaves like an image, automated extraction may be unreliable. DOCX files should also be checked for text boxes and unusual layout objects.

Follow employer instructions

When an employer requests PDF or DOCX, use that format. File naming also matters: use a clear name such as Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf instead of a generic or confusing filename.
