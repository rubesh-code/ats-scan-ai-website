How to Find Resume Keywords in a Job Description

The best keywords are not random industry terms. They are the skills, tools, outcomes, and responsibilities that define the specific role.

ATS Scan AI Editorial TeamUpdated 5 August 20268 min read
Key point

The best keywords are not random industry terms. They are the skills, tools, outcomes, and responsibilities that define the specific role.

Mark repeated requirements

Highlight terms that appear in the title, summary, responsibilities, and qualifications. Repetition often signals that the employer considers the requirement important.

Separate keyword types

Group terms into technical skills, business skills, responsibilities, certifications, domain knowledge, and experience level. This prevents you from treating every word as equally important.

Map each term to evidence

For every important keyword, ask where your resume proves it. The evidence may appear in experience, a project, education, or certification. When no evidence exists, do not add the term as though it does.

Use the employer language naturally

Equivalent wording can be useful. For example, a job description may say “stakeholder management” while your resume says “worked with teams.” Where accurate, use the clearer phrase and explain the actual collaboration.

Prioritize required over preferred

Required skills deserve attention first. Preferred skills can strengthen the resume, but they should not displace core experience or create misleading claims.

Check the final document

After editing, read the resume as a recruiter would. Keywords should appear inside clear achievements and responsibilities, not in an unnatural block designed only for software.
