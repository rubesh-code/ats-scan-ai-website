Is an ATS Score of 80 Good? How to Interpret Resume Scores

A score around 80 can indicate strong alignment in one tool, but scores are not standardized across platforms and do not guarantee an interview.

ATS Scan AI Editorial TeamUpdated 5 August 20266 min read
Key point

A score around 80 can indicate strong alignment in one tool, but scores are not standardized across platforms and do not guarantee an interview.

Scores are tool-specific

Different systems use different criteria, weighting, job descriptions, and extraction methods. An 80 in one product is not directly equivalent to an 80 elsewhere.

Review what created the score

Look at matched skills, missing requirements, formatting observations, and experience evidence. The explanation is more useful than the number alone.

Check critical requirements

A good overall score can hide one important gap, such as a required certification, location, work authorization, or years of experience.

Do not sacrifice accuracy

Adding unsupported keywords may increase a score but weaken the actual application. Recruiters and interviewers can test whether the claimed experience is real.

Compare versions carefully

When revising the resume, change one group of issues at a time. This makes it easier to understand whether stronger evidence, better keywords, or cleaner formatting improved the result.

Use human review too

Ask whether the resume is clear, concise, and convincing to a person. ATS compatibility matters, but recruiters still evaluate relevance, credibility, and communication.
