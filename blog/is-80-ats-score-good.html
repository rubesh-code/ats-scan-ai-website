Is an ATS Score of 80 Good? How to Interpret Resume Scores

Understand what a strong score can indicate, what it cannot guarantee, and which report details matter most.

ATS Scan AI Editorial TeamUpdated 4 August 2026Practical resume guidance

What an 80 ATS score can mean

Within a resume-checking tool, a score around 80 may suggest that the document has strong relevance to the role and relatively few major gaps. It can be a useful signal that the resume includes many of the expected skills and keywords.

However, the meaning depends on the tool, the job description, and the scoring method. An 80 against one job cannot be compared directly with an 80 against another job.

Why ATS scores differ

Employers use different systems and hiring processes. Resume-checking products also use different rules, models, and weightings. There is no universal public score used by every employer, and no score can guarantee that a recruiter will review or select an application.

  • A detailed job description produces a different comparison from a short one.
  • A role requiring unavailable experience may lower the match appropriately.
  • Keyword-heavy resumes can score well while remaining weak for human readers.
  • A strong candidate may score lower because the resume uses different terminology.

Read the report details, not only the number

Review matched skills, missing skills, missing keywords, formatting issues, experience alignment, and improvement suggestions. The strongest report is one that helps you decide what to change and what not to change.

FindingQuestion to askPossible action
Missing keywordDo I genuinely have this skill?Add it with evidence or leave it out
Formatting issueCould the text order be unclear?Simplify the layout and retest
Experience gapIs this role beyond my current level?Target a closer role or build the skill
Weak bulletCan I show scope or outcome?Rewrite using real evidence

What to do after receiving 80

  1. Fix any major parsing or formatting problems.
  2. Review missing required skills and separate wording gaps from real gaps.
  3. Strengthen the most relevant experience bullets.
  4. Check spelling, dates, titles, and contact details.
  5. Read the resume as a recruiter, not only as a scoring tool.
  6. Submit only when every claim is accurate.

Use the score as guidance

The goal is not to reach a perfect number. The goal is to create a resume that accurately communicates why you fit the role. Use the ATS resume checker as one input, then apply human judgment before sending the application.
