Resume keywords

How to Find Resume Keywords in a Job Description

The best resume keywords come from the specific role you are applying for. This guide shows how to turn a job description into a focused checklist and use the terms honestly inside your resume.

ATS Scan AI Editorial TeamUpdated August 4, 20269 min read

Collect repeated terms

Read the job description twice. During the first pass, understand the role. During the second pass, highlight repeated tools, skills, responsibilities, qualifications, and business outcomes. Repetition often indicates importance, but a requirement mentioned once can still be essential.

Pay attention to the job title, opening summary, responsibilities, required qualifications, preferred qualifications, and any technology list.

Group the keywords

Separate the terms into practical categories:

  • Hard skills: React, SQL, financial reporting, recruitment, customer support.
  • Tools: Git, Power BI, Salesforce, Jira, AWS.
  • Responsibilities: analyze data, resolve incidents, manage stakeholders, build APIs.
  • Outcomes: improve conversion, reduce costs, increase reliability, meet compliance requirements.
  • Qualifications: degree, certification, language, years of experience.

Prioritize required items

Create three columns: must have, preferred, and contextual. Must-have items are explicit requirements. Preferred items can improve fit but may not be essential. Contextual terms describe the organization or industry.

Focus first on the must-have items you genuinely possess. Missing a required qualification cannot be solved by repeating nearby keywords.

Match keywords to evidence

For each priority term, write one real example from your background. This transforms a list of words into evidence.

KeywordEvidence questionPossible resume location
Stakeholder managementWhich teams or clients did you coordinate?Experience bullet
SQLWhat did you query, analyze, or automate?Skills and project bullet
Customer retentionWhat action improved retention or reduced churn?Achievement bullet

Place keywords naturally

Use the exact industry term where it is accurate, then write a normal sentence around it. Priority terms can appear in the summary, skills section, experience bullets, and projects. Do not force every term into every section.

Natural placement

Summary: Data analyst experienced in SQL, Power BI, and operational reporting.

Experience: Built Power BI dashboards from SQL datasets to track delivery performance across six regions.

Avoid keyword stuffing

Keyword stuffing makes a resume repetitive and difficult to trust. It can also weaken readability for recruiters. Avoid hidden text, repeated lists, copied paragraphs, or unsupported claims.

Accuracy rule: A keyword belongs in the resume only when you can explain it in an interview and connect it to real work, education, or projects.

Worked example

Suppose a posting requests React, TypeScript, REST APIs, responsive design, Git, testing, and performance optimization.

  1. Mark React, TypeScript, and REST APIs as core technical requirements.
  2. Mark responsive design, testing, and performance optimization as core responsibilities.
  3. Find one project or role showing each capability.
  4. Rewrite bullets to include the capability and result.

Rewritten bullet: Built responsive React and TypeScript interfaces, integrated REST APIs, and improved initial page load performance by 24% through code splitting and asset optimization.
