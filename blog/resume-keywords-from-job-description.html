How to Find Resume Keywords in a Job Description

Extract the right tools, responsibilities, and role language, then add them only where your experience supports them.

ATS Scan AI Editorial TeamUpdated 4 August 2026Practical resume guidance

Step 1: Collect the important requirements

Read the job description once for meaning, then read it again with a highlighter. Mark repeated tools, required qualifications, core responsibilities, and words that describe outcomes. Repetition often signals importance, but a requirement listed once as “must have” may be equally important.

Separate required items from preferred items. Required items deserve priority when they are truthful. Preferred items can strengthen the match but should not replace core qualifications.

Step 2: Group keywords by type

Keyword typeExamplesBest resume location
Tools and platformsReact, AWS, Excel, Power BI, SalesforceSkills and evidence bullets
MethodsAgile, forecasting, A/B testing, CI/CDExperience or project bullets
ResponsibilitiesStakeholder reporting, API integration, data cleaningExperience and projects
OutcomesReduce cost, improve conversion, increase reliabilityAchievement bullets
QualificationsDegree, certification, years of experienceEducation, certifications, summary

Step 3: Map each keyword to evidence

Create two columns: job requirement and your evidence. If the role asks for REST API integration, identify the project or job where you integrated APIs. If the role asks for stakeholder communication, identify the report, presentation, or coordination work that proves it.

Requirement: SQL and dashboard reporting.

Evidence: Used SQL to clean order data and built a weekly Power BI dashboard for sales trends.

When you cannot find honest evidence, do not add the keyword as if you possess the skill. Treat it as a learning gap or decide whether the role is the right match.

Step 4: Place keywords where they make sense

Important terms can appear in the summary, skills section, experience, and projects, but each placement serves a different purpose. Skills show quick relevance. Experience and project bullets prove that the skill was used. A summary can connect the candidate's overall background to the role.

Use both the common abbreviation and full term when helpful, such as “continuous integration and continuous delivery (CI/CD)” or “search engine optimization (SEO).” Do not repeat the same phrase in every section.

Step 5: Avoid keyword stuffing

  • Do not paste the entire job description into the resume.
  • Do not hide white text or invisible keyword blocks.
  • Do not list tools you cannot discuss in an interview.
  • Do not sacrifice readable sentences just to increase repetition.
  • Do not create one generic resume for every job.

After the keywords are placed naturally, run the document through the ATS resume checker and read the missing-keyword section together with the experience-match feedback.
