Resume format

ATS-Friendly Resume Format: Structure, Headings, and Examples

An ATS-friendly resume should be easy for both software and people to read. The strongest format is usually simple, consistent, and focused on evidence rather than decorative design.

ATS Scan AI Editorial TeamUpdated August 4, 202611 min read

Choose a simple layout

Start with a single-column document when you do not know which applicant tracking system the employer uses. A single reading path reduces ambiguity and makes it easier to follow titles, employers, dates, and achievements in the intended order.

Use comfortable margins, a readable font, and enough spacing between sections. The resume should still look professional, but decoration should never carry essential meaning.

Use standard section headings

Common headings are easier to recognize than creative alternatives. Use headings such as Professional Summary, Skills, Work Experience, Education, Projects, and Certifications. Avoid replacing “Work Experience” with unusual labels such as “My Journey” or “Where I Made an Impact.”

Recommended order

  1. Name and contact information
  2. Professional summary
  3. Core skills
  4. Work experience
  5. Projects, when relevant
  6. Education
  7. Certifications

Write a focused summary

The summary should identify your profession, level of experience, strongest relevant capabilities, and the type of value you offer. Keep it concise and tailor it to the target role.

Example: Frontend developer with four years of experience building responsive React and TypeScript applications, integrating REST APIs, and improving performance for customer-facing platforms.

Organize the skills section

Group related skills instead of presenting an unstructured keyword wall. For a technical role, categories might include Languages, Frameworks, Databases, Cloud, Testing, and Tools. For an operations role, use categories such as Process Improvement, Reporting, Customer Support, Systems, and Compliance.

Only list capabilities you can discuss confidently. Important skills should also appear naturally in experience or project evidence.

Format work experience clearly

For every position, show job title, employer, location when useful, and dates in a consistent arrangement. Follow with three to six focused bullets, giving more space to recent and relevant work.

ElementGood practice
Job titleUse the official title or a truthful clarifying equivalent.
EmployerUse the full organization name.
DatesChoose one format, such as Jan 2023–Present.
BulletsLead with actions and include tools, scope, and results.

Achievement bullet pattern

Action + work performed + tool or method + result.

Example: Automated weekly operations reporting in Excel and Power Query, reducing manual preparation time from five hours to ninety minutes.

Keep dates and titles consistent

Use the same date format throughout the document. Do not switch between “2023–2025,” “March 2022 to Current,” and “06/21–08/22.” Consistency improves readability and reduces confusion.

Place promotions as separate roles under one employer when responsibilities changed significantly. This shows progression without repeating the company information unnecessarily.

Formatting choices to avoid

  • Essential contact details placed only in headers or footers.
  • Multiple narrow columns with complex reading order.
  • Important skills communicated only through icons or charts.
  • Tables used to contain the entire resume.
  • Images containing text that must be read.
  • Unusual fonts or very small body text.
  • Inconsistent bullets, headings, and date styles.

A modest visual accent is acceptable when the main information remains plain text and logically ordered.

Final ATS-friendly resume checklist

  • The target role is clear.
  • Standard section headings are used.
  • Contact information is plain text.
  • Important keywords are supported by evidence.
  • Dates follow one consistent format.
  • Experience bullets show actions and outcomes.
  • The document has been proofread manually.
  • The selected file type follows the employer’s instruction.
